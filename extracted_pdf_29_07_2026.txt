Research and Construction of a Vietnamese SMS Dataset 
for Machine Learning Applications. 
Abstract. The scarcity of high -quality, transparently verified benchmarks se-
verely bottlenecks the development of intelligent Vietnamese SMS spam and 
fraudulent detection systems. To address the issue of label noise and poor re-
producibility in existing corpora, thi s paper introduces a quality-assured dataset 
construction pipeline featuring a multi -annotator validation framework and a 
structured disagreement resolution workflow. Within the current project phase, 
2,058 raw messages were integrated from multiple source s, with a subset of 
2,007 messages successfully completing the entire evaluation pipeline. Prior to 
formal adjudication, the independent labeling phase involving ten unique hu-
man annotators achieved an overall Fleiss' Kappa coefficient of 0.9856, signify-
ing an almost perfect baseline agreement and validating the robustness of the 
proposed annotation guideline. Prior to model evaluation, an additional near -
duplicate filtering stage was performed to remove redundant message templates 
originating from both mass spam campaigns and automated transactional notifi-
cations (e.g., banking alerts and OTP messages) that could cause train -test leak-
age under cross -validation. This produced an experimental subset of 1,541 
unique messages for benchmark evaluation using stra tified five -fold cross -
validation

1 Introduction 
Short Message Service (SMS) remains an essential communication channel in Vi-
etnam for banking, governmental services, and commercial transactions. According to 
DataReportal, Vietnam recorded approximately 137 million mobile cellular subscrip - 
tions in 2026, significantly exceeding the national population and highlighting the 
widespread adoption of mobile services [1]. However, this ubiquity has led to a per -
sistent rise in SMS spam, creating major cybersecurity challenges. Attackers increas - 
ingly employ sophisticated social engineering techniqu es, impersonating banks and 
trusted organizations to deceive users into revealing sensitive data [3, 4]. Consequent - 
ly, developing reliable automated detection systems has attracted substantial attention 
from both academia and industry. 
Although significa nt progress has been made in Vietnamese SMS classification, 
the availability of high -quality benchmark datasets remains limited. Most existing 
public corpora, such as the SMS Spam Collection [6], were developed for English and 
do not represent the linguist ic nuances, localized abbr eviations, or specific scam pat-
terns observed in Vietnamese. Furthermore, existing r egional datasets are often lim-
ited in scale, proprietary, or lack transparent documentation regarding annotation

quality control. Many lack clear disagreement resolution procedures, which under -
mines dataset reproducibility, annotation consistency, and long -term research value 
[7]. 
To address these limitations, this paper presents a quality -assured, transparent 
framework for constructing a Vietnames e SMS dataset. Rather than relying solely on 
simplistic majority voting, our methodology prioriti zes annotation integrity by com-

establish high-fidelity Gold Labels. Messa ges reaching a consensus among ten inde-

messages, successfully validating and finalizing a baseline subset of 2,007 high-
quality Vietnamese SMS records for downstream machine learning research.  
2 Related work 
While substantial efforts have been made to construct benchmark corpora, existing 
datasets exhibit significant limitations regarding annotation transparency and quality 
control. For instance, the widely used SMS Spam Collection v.1 for English (Almeida 
et al.) and the prior Vietnamese dataset proposed by Pham and Le -Hong (2016) rely 
on manual aggregation but fail to report formal Inter -Annotator Agreement (IAA) 
metrics or structured disagreement resolution protocols. This lack of transparent 
quality assurance undermines dataset reproducibility and the reliability of the ground -
truth labels. Furthermore, existing works often overlook the critical issue of near -
duplicate template -based campaigns, which can lead to train -test leakage and artifi-
cially inflated model performance. In contrast, our proposed dataset directly addresses 
these gaps by establishing a rigorous multi -annotator framework (achieving a Fleiss' 
Kappa of 0.9856) and a strict deduplication pipeline prior to benchmark evaluation

3 Proposed Dataset Construction Framework 
3.1 Overall Framework 
 
Fig. 1. Overall workflow of the proposed quality-assured Vietnamese SMS dataset construction 
framework. 
The primary objective of this study is not  only to construct a Vietnamese SMS dataset 
but also to establish a quality -assured and reproducible annotation framework for 
generating reliable ground -truth labels. Instead of treating annotation as a one -time 
labeling activity, the proposed framework in corporates continuous quality improve -
ment throughout the dataset construction process. The overall w orkflow is illustrated 
in Fig. 1. 
The proposed framework consists of four major stages: SMS data collection, anno -
tation preparation, independent multi -annotator labeling, and iterative quality assur -
ance. SMS messages are first collected from multiple heterogeneous sources and fil -
tered according to predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Prior to annotation, all 
annotators are provided with a standard ized Annotation Guideline to ensure a com -
mon understanding of annotation objectives and decision rules. 
Inspired by the systematic disagreement resolution procedure proposed by 
Oortwijn et al. [7], the proposed framework treats annotation disagreements as  indica-
tors of potential quality issues rather than simple annotation errors. Each disagree-
ment is therefore analyzed to identify its underlying cause, including ambiguous an-
notation guidlines, conceptual misalignment among annotators, inconsistent annota-
tion behavior, or individual annotation mistakes. Appropriate corrective actions, such 
as guideline refinement, annotator retraining, or re -annotation, are subsequently per-
formed before the messages are re evaluated through the verification process. 
Unlike conventional annotation pipelines, the proposed framework establishes a 
closed-loop quality assurance mechanism, in which disagreement analysis continu -
ously improves both the annotation guideline and annotator consistency. This iterative 
process aims to m aximize annotation reliability while ensuring that the resulting da -

taset remains transparent, reproducible, and suitable for future machine learning and 
natural language processing research. 
3.2 SMS Collection 
The quality of a machine learning dataset largely  depends on the diversity, authentici -
ty, and representativeness of its data sources. Therefore, the proposed dataset was 
constructed by collecting Vietnamese SMS messages from multiple heterogeneous 
sources rather than relying on a single collection chann el. This strategy aims to im -
prove linguistic diversity while reducing potential sampling bias. 
 
 
Fig. 2. SMS Data Collection Pipeline 
As illustrated in Fig. 2, SMS messages were collected from four primary channels: 
community contributions, volunteer submissions, publicly available online resources, 
and partner organizations. In the dataset schema (Table 4), these sources are mapped 
into three standardized categorical attributes: crawl (online resources), user_report 
(community and volunteer submissions), and whitelist (partner organizations). Com-
munity contributions and volunteer submissions provide real -world messages re-
ceived by mobile users in their daily lives, allowing the dataset to capture contempo-
rary phishing  campaigns, promotional advertisements, financial notifications, and 
legitimate personal communications.  
To protect user privacy, all collected messages underwent an anonymization pro-
cessbefore annotation. Personally identifiable information (PII), includ ing phone 
numbers, personal names, identification numbers, bank account information, URLs, 
and othersensitive content, was either removed or replaced with standardized place -
holders while preserving the semantic characteristics required for annotation and sub-
sequent machine learning applications. 
To ensure high data quality and eliminate potential noise before the formal annota -
tion phase, a rigorous screening pipeline was executed combining automated scripts  
and a systematic manual inspection protocol. Ou t of the 2,058 raw messages initially-

3.3 Annotation Guideline 
To minimize annotation subjectivity and improve labeling consistency, a standardized 
Annotation Guideline was developed before the annotation process. The guideline 
establishes a unified decision -making framework by defining annotation objectives, 
label definitions, decision rules, and representative examples. All annotators were 
required to study the guideline before participating in the annotation task. 
The guideline also addresses key linguistic nuances in Vietnamese SMS communi-
cation. As observed by Tuan et al. [4], spammers overwhelmingly favor unaccented 
text (non -diacritic Vietnamese) to bypass  character limit constraints and reduce 
transmission costs while preserving readability for human targets. Our guideline in-
corporates standard normalization rules (e.g., mapping variant promotional markers 
such as 'quảng cáo', 'quang cao', '[QC]' to a unif ied token representation [4]) and spec-
ifies decision rules for ambiguous cases. Annotators were instructed to evaluate each 
message according to its communication intent, contextual meaning, and potential 
security risk rather than isolated keywords. For ex ample, the presence of terms such 
as promotion, bank, or reward does not automatically indicate spam. Instead, annota-
tors considered whether the overall message exhibited deception, impersonation, co-
ercion, or unauthorized commercial solicitation. 
Spam is defined as a broad category comprising two semantic sub -categories: 
Comercial Spam, which includes unsolicited marketing promotions and unauthorized

advertisements, and Fraudulent/Phishing Spam, which includes impersonation at-
tacks, fraudulent financial sc hemes, and malicious messages intended to steal infor-
mation or financial assets. 
For the baseline binary classification task, both sub -categories are assigned the 
same Spam label. This design reflects the objective of constructing a binary SMS 
spam detection dataset while preserving finer semantic distinctions in the annotation 
guideline for future analyses an d potential taxonomy extension. The guideline also 
specifies decision rules for ambiguous cases. Annotators were instructed to evaluate 
each message a ccording to its communication intent, contextual meaning, and poten-

Representative examples and common annotation pitfalls identified during  prelim-
inary annotation rounds were incorporated into the guideline to improve inter -
annotator consistency. During the verification stage, the guideline was revised when -
ever systematic ambiguities were identified through disagreement analysis, supporting 
continuous improvement of annotation quality. 
3.4 Independent Multi-Annotator Labeling 
After completing the annotation preparation stage, the collected SMS messages were 
independently annotated by a team of trained annotators following the standardized 
Annotation Guideline described in the previous section. Independent annotat ion was 
adopted to minimize potential bias arising from communication among annotators and 
to ensure that each labeling decision reflected the annotator's own interpretation of the  
predefined annotation criteria. 
Each SMS message was assigned to ten indepe ndent annotators, all of whom eval-
uated the message without access to the labels assigned by other annotators. This 
independent annotation strategy provides multiple judgments for each message, al-
lowing the subsequent verification process to evaluate annot ation consistency and 
identify disagreement cases objectively. The annotation team comprised 10 native 
Vietnamese speakers (undergraduate and graduate researchers in Computer Science 
and Cybersecurity). All annotators completed a formal training session on  the Anno-
tation Guideline prior to labeling. Annotators were compensated fairly for their effort 
and signed privacy non-disclosure agreements to protect user data. 
Unlike majority-voting approaches that determine the final label immediately after 
annotation, the proposed framework intentionally separates annotation from verifica-
tion. The objective of this design is to preserve all individual annotation decisions for 
subsequent quality assessment rather than discarding minority opinions. Consequent-
ly, every annotation record, including disagreement cases, becomes valuable evidence 
for evaluating annotation consistency and identifying potential weakn esses in the 
annotation process. 
After all annotators completed their labeling tasks, the annotation results wer e 
merged into a unified annotation repository. At this stage, no annotation was manual-

ly modified or removed. Instead, the merged annotation results served as the input for 
the dual-stage verification framework presented in the following section. 
3.5 Consensus Assessment and Disagreement Resolution 
Following independent annotation, results are subjected to a structured consensus 
assessment to evaluate consistency. Unlike conventional workflows that rely on ma-
jority voting, the proposed framework preserves every  individual decision and sys-
tematically examines disagreement cases to identify quality issues. 
Messages achieving complete consensus are directly accepted into the final dataset. 
Conversely, disagreement cases are transferred to a dedicated analysis proce ss. In-
spired by Oortwijn et al. [7], this analysis evaluates multiple perspectives, including 
guideline ambiguity, conceptual inconsistency, variable annotation behavior, and 
human error, to address root causes systematically rather than case-by-case. 
3.6 Ethics Statement and Data Licensing 
Ethics Statement: All SMS messages were collected with user consent or obtained 
from public reporting platforms. Sensitive personal details —including phone num-
bers, personal names, account credentials, and specific URLs —were systematically 
replaced with standardized placeholders (e.g., [PHONE], [URL], [NAME]) during 
anonymization to safeguard user privacy.

4.2 Inter-Annotator Agreement 
Following the first-stage annotation process, each SMS message was independent-
ly labeled by ten annotators according to the Annotation Guideline described in Sec-
tion 2. To evaluate annotation consistency, the individual annotation files were 
merged by SMS identifier, allowing the labels assigned by different annotator s to be 
aligned and compared on a message-by-message basis. 
The merged annotation matrix was evaluated using Fleiss' Kappa, a statistical 
measure designed to assess agreement among multiple annotators assigning categori-
cal labels to the same set of instanc es [8, 10]. Unlike Cohen's Kappa, which is math-
ematically restricted to a fixed pair of exactly two raters [5, 9], Cohen's framework is 
fundamentally unsuitable for this study because each SMS message was independent-
ly annotated by a larger group of ten an notators. Consequently, Fleiss' Kappa is 
uniquely appropriate as it accommodates an arbitrary number of raters per item and 
estimates the degree to which the observed consensus exceeds the chance -expected 
agreement [8].

4.4 Baseline Classification Results 
4.4.1. Experimental Setup and Implementation Details 
To establish a transparent, highly reproducible performance benchmark for Vi-
etnam-ese SMS spam detection, we conducted a comparative empirical study using 
the fi -nalized Gold Standard corpus. Before model training, a dedicated near -
duplicate fil -tering stage was applied to remove template -based SMS messages that 
could intro -duce train-test leakage. The resulting evaluation corpus contained 1,541 
unique SMS messages. The classification workflow evaluates two distinct families of 
algorithms: traditional machine learning architectures utilizing surface -level features 
and a fine-tuned deep contextual Transformer model [11]. 
For traditional machine learning baselines, including Support Vector Machine with 
Linear and RBF kernels [12], Logistic Regression [13], Multinomial Naive Bayes 
[14], and a Multi -Layer Perceptron [15], textual representations were extracted using 
Term Frequency -Inverse Document Frequency (TF -IDF) vectorization implemented 
via scikit-learn [16]. The feature space was configured with an n -gram range of (1, 2) 
and capped at a maximum vocabulary size of 5,000 features. Prior to feature extrac-
tion, Vietnamese compound words were segmented using the PyVi toolkit (ViTo-
kenizer.tokenize) [17]. However, because PyVi relies on standard dictionary lookup, 


--- PAGE 13 ---
 
 
its segmentation efficacy degrades on unaccented SMS text and informal tee ncode 
(e.g., 'ck' for 'chuyển khoản', 'ngkhac' for 'người khác'), introducing tokenization noise 
into traditional surface feature representations. 
To fulfill our promise of evaluating deep pre -trained language models, we integrat -
ed vinai/phobert -base (Pho BERT-base), a state -of-the-art pre -trained Transformer 
architecture optimized for Vietnamese natural language processing. Fine -tuning of 
PhoBERT-base was executed on a dedicated workstation equipped with an NVIDIA 
GeForce RTX 3060 GPU running Python 3.14.2  and PyTorch 2.12.0+cu126. The 
model was trained for 4 epochs using the  AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 
0.00002 (2e-5), a weight decay of 0.01, a warmup ratio of 0.1, a per -device batch size 
of 16, and a maximum sequence length of 128 tokens. Model  selection was governed 
by evaluating the highest F1 -score on the positive class at each epoch 
(load_best_model_at_end=True). 
4.4.2. Near-Duplicate Filtering and Train-Test Leakage Protection 
While the deduplication process was initially motivated by the n eed to filter out 
template-based phishing and spam campaigns, empirical observations revealed that 
the vast majority of near -duplicates (87%) were actually legitimate messages (Ham). 
This is highly consistent with real -world mobile traffic, where legitima te institutional 
communications, such as banking OTPs, automated transaction aler ts, and promotion-
al broadcasts, rely heavily on fixed templates. 
To eliminate potential evaluation bias caused by near -duplicate spam campaigns, a 
rigorous multi-stage deduplication pipeline was executed on the 2,007 gold -standard 
messages prior to any dataset splitting. Phishing campaigns typically generate vast 
amounts of template -based messages that differ only in dynamic entities (e.g., recipi -
ent names, phone numbers, or fr audulent URLs). Performing cross -validation directly 
on raw data would risk placing near -identical variants into both training and testing 
folds, leading to artificially inflated performance metrics.  To prevent this, we em-
ployed a character-level 3-gram Jaccard similarity metric. Texts were first normalized 
(lowercased and whitespace -trimmed) and decomposed into sets of contiguous 3 -
character sequences. The Jaccard index between two mes -sage sets 𝑆𝐴  and 𝑆𝐵 was 
computed as:  
 𝐽(𝑆𝐴, 𝑆𝐵) =
|𝑆𝐴∩𝑆𝐵|
|𝑆𝐴∪𝑆𝐵| (2) 
Messages exhibiting a similarity threshold of 𝐽 ≥ 0.85 were identified as campaign 
variants and filtered out. This stage successfully removed 466 near -duplicate records, 
yielding a strictly unique benchmark corpus of 1,541 messages (878 Ham  and 663 
Spam). This workflow guarantees that no message pair with 𝐽 ≥ 0.85 spans across the 
training and evaluation splits of any fold.

learning baselines, SVM with RBF kernel achieved the highest overall recall (98.04%

contextual embeddings (PhoBERT) significantly outperform traditional surface -level 
feature models (such as Naive Bayes and SVM) across diverse Vietnamese text vari-

tokenization and deep contextual attention, enabling it to map noisy teencode variants 
to their underlying semantic representations effectively within a strictly deduplicated, 
leakage-controlled evaluation framework. 
4.5 Limitations 
While the proposed framework establishes a transparent, quality -assured dataset for 
Vietnamese SMS classification, several research limitations should be acknowledged:  
1. In-Distribution Evaluation Scope: Model performance metrics evaluate five -fold 
cross-validation on an in -distribution, deduplicated benchmark corpus. Real -world 
performance on out -of-distribution (OOD) traffic or newly emerging zero -day 
phishing campaigns employing novel lexical obfuscation tactics may vary. 
2. Dataset Scale and Longitudinal Expansion:  The dataset currently encapsulates 
2,007 gold -standard messages (1,541 unique records). Although sufficient for es-
tablishing robust baseline classifiers, continuous longitudinal collection is neces-
sary to track evolving mobile threats over time. 
3. Morphological Segmentation Bottlenecks: Standard Vietnamese segmenters (e.g., 
PyVi) exhibit reduced accuracy on non -diacritic SMS text and informal teencode, 
emphasizing the necessity of domain-adapted subword language models.

5 Conclusion and future work 
This study establishes a quality -assured and transparent annotation methodology for 
constructing a Vietnamese SMS phishing dataset, shifting the emphasis from simple 
data accumulation to reproducible quality control. Rather than dismissing human 
disagreement as stati stical noise, the proposed framework leverages annotator diver-
gence as a critical linguistic signal to uncover semantic ambiguities and systematical-
ly refine the underlying gold standard labels. Prior to formal adjudication, the inde-
pendent labeling phase involving ten unique annotators achieved an overall Fleiss' 
Kappa coefficient of 0.9856, signifying an 'Almost Perfect Agreement.' This high 
baseline consensus confirms that the standardized annotation guidelines effectively 
established clear decision boun daries for the vast majority of messages. Human disa-
greement was strictly confined to a minor 2.19% subset of edge cases, primarily 
caused by non -standard banking notification templates and localized teencode abbre-
viations. By routing these specific border line instances through the structured disa-
greement resolution workflow, the methodology effectively guarantees the integrity 
of the finalized dataset. 
At the current developmental stage, the corpus comprises 2,058 collected messag-
es, of which 2,007 have successfully progressed through the complete multi -annotator 
validation pipeline (yielding 1,541 unique records post -deduplication). To ensure 
absolute transparency and facilitate open -science collaboration, the final benchmark 
dataset is packaged alongside its standardized Annotation Guideline, granular disa-
greement metadata, and open -access distribution formats. Future initiatives will ac-
tively expand this corpus beyond its current phase to achieve a targeted milestone of 
over 5,000 annotated Vietnamese SMS  messages, specifically focusing on capturing 
newly emerging zero -day phishing variants to stress -test and evolve the resolution 
framework continuously. Ultimately, this scaling corpus serves as a foundational, 
high-fidelity benchmark for training and eval uating next-generation intelligent cyber-
security detection systems within the Vietnamese natural language processing do-
main.
